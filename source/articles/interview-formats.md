# Phone screens, panels and case interviews

**Covers:** Phone screen, Panel interview, Case interview

## One job, several kinds of interview {#overview}
Few hiring processes use a single conversation. Companies add steps to save time or to test different skills. Knowing what each step is for tells you how to prepare. A step that checks basic fit needs a clear summary of your background. A step with many people needs a way to share eye contact. A step with a problem needs a calm method.

## Phone screen {#phone-screen}
A phone screen is a short first call, often 15 to 30 minutes, usually with a recruiter. The goal is to check whether you meet the basics: your experience, your pay expectations, your availability and your interest.

Treat it as a real interview. Be in a quiet place, keep your resume in front of you, and have a one-minute summary ready. Smile while you talk, because it changes your voice. Ask what the next step is before you hang up.

## Panel interview {#panel-interview}
A panel interview has several interviewers asking questions in one meeting. They may be from different teams, so each cares about something different: the manager about results, a peer about teamwork, a human resources person about the company rules.

Look at the person who asked the question as you start your answer, then let your eyes move around the whole group as you continue. Write down every name when you are introduced. Mention a name when you refer back to a question: "As Priya asked earlier..." Thank everyone at the end.

## Case interview {#case-interview}
In a case interview you get a business problem and must work through it aloud. A typical prompt is "A coffee shop's sales dropped by 10 percent. What would you do?" Nobody expects a perfect answer. The interviewer is listening to how you think.

Use a simple method. Repeat the problem to check you understood. Ask clarifying questions. Split the problem into parts, such as customers, prices and costs. Test each part with a quick calculation. Finish with a recommendation and one risk.

## A worked example {#example}
A short case: a coffee shop sells 500 cups a day at 4 dollars each, and sales fell by 10 percent.

1. Today's sales: 500 cups times 4 dollars is 2,000 dollars a day.
2. A 10 percent fall is 200 dollars a day, which is about 50 cups.
3. Ask: did the number of customers drop, or did each customer buy less?
4. If a new shop opened nearby, test a loyalty card. If prices rose, test a smaller size at the old price.
5. Recommend the cheapest test first and say what number would prove it worked.

The numbers are small on purpose. What matters is the steps: clarify, split, calculate, recommend.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Staying silent while you think. In all three formats, the interviewer cannot read your mind. On the phone and in a case, silence feels longer than it is. Say "Let me think for a moment" or narrate what you are considering.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A phone screen checks basic fit, so be ready with a clear summary and a quiet space.
- In a panel interview, answer to everyone and use their names.
- A case interview rewards a clear method more than a perfect answer.
- Think aloud, so the interviewer can follow you.
