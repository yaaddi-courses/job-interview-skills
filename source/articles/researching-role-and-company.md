# Researching the role and the company

**Covers:** Job description, LinkedIn, Culture fit

## Why research comes first {#overview}
Most interview questions are really one question in disguise: why are you right for this job and this company? You can only answer that well if you know what the job needs and what the company is like. Research turns vague answers such as "I am a hard worker" into specific ones such as "you need someone to handle returns quickly, and I did exactly that for two years."

Good research does not take long. An hour is enough for most interviews, and it usually pays off in the first five minutes.

## Job description {#job-description}
Treat the posting as the interviewer's own checklist. The people who wrote it listed what they will test.

Read it twice. The first time, split each line into two groups: what the job requires and what would be nice to have. The required items are the ones you must show evidence for. The second time, underline the verbs and tools, such as "schedule", "negotiate" or "spreadsheet", and note where in your own history you have done each one.

Words repeated in the posting matter most. If "teamwork" appears four times, expect a question about it, and prepare an example before you walk in.

## LinkedIn {#linkedin}
LinkedIn is the quickest way to put faces and careers behind the names. Open the company page to see how large the team is and what it has announced lately. Look up the person you will meet, if you know their name. Notice how long they have been there, where they worked before, and what they write about.

Use what you find to ask better questions and to find common ground, such as a shared school or a former employer. Do not mention small personal details that make the interviewer feel watched. Also check your own profile: its dates and job titles should match your resume exactly, because many interviewers will look at both.

## Culture fit {#culture-fit}
Companies want people who will work well with the team they already have. That is what culture fit means: how closely your values and working style match the place.

You can learn a lot before the interview. Read the careers page and the company values, look at how staff describe their work, and notice the tone of the job posting. Is it formal or relaxed? Does it praise independence or collaboration? Then pick two or three true stories from your own life that match those values.

Culture fit does not mean being the same as everyone. It means showing that you understand how the company works and that you would be comfortable there.

## A worked example {#example}
A posting for a customer support specialist asks for "calm communication", "fast problem solving" and "use of a ticket system". Here is how one candidate turns it into a plan:

| The posting says | My evidence | Where I will say it |
|---|---|---|
| Calm communication | Handled a refund dispute at a shop without raising my voice | "Tell me about a difficult customer" |
| Fast problem solving | Cut average reply time from 6 to 4 hours | "What are you proud of?" |
| Ticket system | Used a help desk tool for two years | The opening introduction |

The company page says "we value honesty with customers". The candidate adds one more line: a time they told a customer the truth about a delay, and what happened next. Nothing in the table is invented. It only organizes what is already true.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Reading the posting once, nodding, and moving on. Without a list that links each requirement to a real example, you will meet the question with a blank mind. Write the table down, even on a single sheet of paper, and say each answer aloud once.

## Key points {#key-points}
- The job description tells you what the interviewer will test. Match each required item to your own evidence.
- LinkedIn gives you context on the company and the people. Use it to ask good questions, and keep your own profile consistent with your resume.
- Culture fit is about showing that you understand and would enjoy the way the team works, not about being identical to it.
- An hour of research is enough if it ends with a written plan.
